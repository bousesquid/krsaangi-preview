Benchmark

Home pages of the four named competitors were reviewed alongside the live Krsaangi site. What each does well, and where Krsaangi can pass them.

KanakavalliCalm, photographic, confident. The closest in tone. But products are named by stock code, so a customer cannot tell two saris apart without opening them, and discovery depends on knowing the house’s own collectives.
Tulsi WeavesStrong house sub-brands and a visible WhatsApp contact. The home page is a long run of identical carousels with colour-plus-fabric titles, so nothing reads as special.
ShobitamThe widest range and useful services such as wedding planning and trunk shows. Breadth costs it focus; it reads as a marketplace.
Chettinad HandloomClear category entry points. A slideshow home page with little about craft or availability.
Krsaangi todayExcellent, distinctive collections with real ideas behind them (Pazhamai, Kattam, Snigdha). The theme hides them: a hamburger menu on desktop, template footer copy, no filters, and sold-out and in-stock pieces mixed without distinction.
Where this design leadsPlain-language search, colour in the weaver’s vocabulary, availability that is never ambiguous, product pages that explain the piece, and recommendations that state their reason.

Filter taxonomy

Each filter maps to one structured field. A filter is shown only when the current page has at least two live values for it, so customers never meet an empty or unreliable control.

Colour system

Every product keeps its exact traditional colour name. Each name also belongs to one family, which is what filters and plain-language search use. Aliases widen discovery without renaming anything: “dark red” finds Arakku and Maroon, and the card still says Arakku.

Body and border are stored separately, so “pink saree with green border” means exactly that. Shot colours and other names that carry meaning should be reviewed by someone who knows the textiles before the vocabulary is frozen.

Naming

Format: Collection — key design or motif — body colour × border colour. The collection is the eyebrow, the design is the title, and the colour pair sits beneath it, so cards align and the eye finds the same fact in the same place. Traditional terms are kept as written.

Image template

One ratio everywhere, 3:4, shot so the border and pallu are never cropped. The same order on every product means the second image on a card is always the full saree.

01 Cover02 Full saree03 Draped front04 Alternate angle05 Pallu06 Border07 Weave close-up08 Zari or motif09 Blouse piece10 Texture11 Video

Images are served from the Shopify CDN at the width each layout asks for, in WebP or AVIF, with lazy loading below the fold. Many current uploads are HEIC straight from a phone; they convert correctly but a short capture guide would make the catalogue consistent.

Publishing workflow

DraftContent reviewImage reviewReady to publishScheduledLiveSold outArchived

A product cannot move to Ready to publish while any of these is missing: images, price, colour, weave, fabric, inventory, description, SEO title and description. In Shopify this is a workflow.status metafield with a Flow automation that blocks publishing and lists what is missing. Sold-out pieces stay visible with their status until archived, so links never break.

Search & Ask us a question

Both run on the same idea: turn ordinary words into the structured fields above, then retrieve. Neither writes facts of its own.

  • Search reads price, availability, zari, body colour, border colour, weave, collection, category, motif and border from the query, corrects spelling against the catalogue’s own vocabulary, and shows what it understood as chips.
  • Curation rules cover words the catalogue does not tag yet. “Wedding” looks at Kanjivaram silks with zari; “vintage” at Pazhamai and Maathey; “lightweight” at Gadwal, silk cotton, cotton and khadi. Each rule is shown to the customer, and each should be replaced by real occasion tags.
  • If nothing matches exactly, search relaxes one constraint at a time and says which.
  • Ask us a question in this prototype is rule-based so it can be tested offline. In production it is Claude with two tools, catalogue search and policy lookup, and one instruction that cannot be overridden: answer only from tool results, otherwise say “I don’t have verified information about that” and offer the team or WhatsApp.
  • Order questions need a signed-in customer and the Shopify customer account API. Until that is connected, order questions link to the Shopify account.

Analytics

One track(event, properties) call feeds a data layer. No personal data is attached; consent gates the forwarders.

Architecture

The recommendation is to stay on Shopify and build this as a custom Online Store 2.0 theme, not a headless storefront. Everything here, including the filters, predictive search, cart drawer and quick view, is achievable with Liquid sections, metafields, the Search & Discovery app and a small amount of JavaScript. That keeps checkout, customer accounts, apps and the team’s editing workflow intact, and it is cheaper to maintain. Go headless with Hydrogen or Next.js only if the theme cannot hold the performance targets.

  • This prototype is static HTML, one stylesheet and four small scripts with no framework or build step, so it can be read as a specification. Components map one-to-one to theme sections and snippets.
  • Ask us a question is the one piece that needs a server: a small endpoint that holds the API key and exposes the two tools.
  • Structured data is in place for Organization, WebSite, Product and BreadcrumbList. On Shopify these render on the server.

Needed from Krsaangi

  • Shipping, delivery time, returns and care text, so the pending slots can be filled.
  • Preorder terms: payment, cancellation and typical weaving time.
  • WhatsApp channel link and WhatsApp number.
  • A decision on a review app, and one test order to confirm payment end to end.
  • The logo as a vector file, and a light-background version if one exists.
  • Film shot for the site: sarees in motion, weaving, zari in the light. The current films are phone clips.
  • The brand story in the founder’s words, to replace the story block assembled from collection copy.
  • A review of the derived colours and attributes, especially shot colours and unusual names.
  • Customer photographs with permission, and confirmation of which reviews may be shown.
